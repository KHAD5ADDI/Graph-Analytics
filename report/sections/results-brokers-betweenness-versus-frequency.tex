Table~\ref{tab:brokers} compares the leading ingredients under hop and
$1/\mathrm{PMI}$ distances. With hops, Water, Salt and Butter lead the
ranking. Across all ingredients, the Spearman correlation between frequency
and hop betweenness is $\rho=0.968$. Degree also follows frequency
($\rho=0.955$). Common ingredients are the path intermediaries in this
graph. We read these correlations descriptively, because frequency and
edges come from the same recipes.

Of the $65{,}341$ ingredient pairs, $16.61\%$ are one hop apart,
$81.51\%$ two hops and $1.88\%$ three hops. Hop betweenness therefore mostly
counts how often an ingredient is a common neighbour. It closely follows
degree ($\rho=0.973$). Water's normalised value is $0.04094$: averaged over
pairs of other ingredients, $4.09\%$ of shortest paths pass through Water.

With $1/\mathrm{PMI}$ distances, the leaders become Couscous, Lasagna,
Shallot, Coconut and Saffron. The Spearman correlation with frequency falls
to $\rho=0.323$. We interpret these ingredients as brokers under this
distance choice only. Stronger PMI associations give shorter steps, so
these shortest paths need not use the most frequent ingredients.

\paragraph{Betweenness beyond degree.}
Among the hop top $50$, the largest rank gaps belong to Milk, Egg, Flour,
Orange and Cream. Their degree ranks and hop-betweenness ranks are respectively
$55\to25$, $41\to15$, $43\to17$, $47\to21$ and $27\to5$.
Their shares of neighbours outside their community are $37.5\%$, $36.9\%$,
$40.5\%$, $42.1\%$ and $51.8\%$. All belong to community $1$, the sweet
and baking group. They link this group to others, although the shares alone
do not identify which groups. These rank differences show that degree does
not fully determine hop betweenness.

\paragraph{A second distance conversion.}
The $1/\mathrm{PMI}$ and $1/(1+\mathrm{PMI})$ rankings correlate strongly
($\rho=0.919$), sharing $6$ of their top $10$ ingredients. With the second
conversion, Couscous ranks $2$, Shallot $1$, Coconut $3$ and Saffron $6$;
they remain leading brokers. Lasagna falls from $2$ to $26$, leaving the
top $10$. The correlation with frequency rises to $\rho=0.594$, below
the hop result. Thus several leading weighted brokers survive, but their
exact order and the weak association with frequency do not. This supports
only partial robustness to the two conversions tested.

\paragraph{Inspecting individual ingredients.}
Community numbers refer to Table~\ref{tab:communities}. Water has degree
$189$, with $72$ neighbours inside community $1$ and $117$ outside. It ranks
$1$ with hops but $332$ with $1/\mathrm{PMI}$. Its strongest PMI partners
include Yeast, Meat bouillon, Gelatin, Gin and Bulgur, linking baking,
setting mixtures, drinks, broth and grain ingredients.

Shallot occurs in $2{,}101$ recipes and ranks $31$ with hops but $3$ with
$1/\mathrm{PMI}$. It has $77$ neighbours inside community $0$ and $78$ outside. Its
strongest PMI partners include Chanterelle, Chervil, Oyster mushroom,
Madeira wine and Cognac Brandy, connecting mushrooms, a herb and cooking
alcohols. Oyster mushroom belongs to community $3$; the others belong to $0$.

Couscous occurs in only $245$ recipes. It ranks $175$ with hops but $1$
with $1/\mathrm{PMI}$. It has $28$ neighbours inside community $0$ and $26$
outside. Its strongest PMI partners are Turnip, Vegetable Broth, Apricot,
Butternut squash and Currant. The vegetables and broth belong to community
$0$; the fruit belongs to $1$. Couscous thus links vegetable and broth
ingredients with fruit.

The ingredient graph has no articulation point and no bridge, so no single
ingredient or edge holds it together. Betweenness therefore measures how
much an ingredient brokers paths, not a single point of failure.
