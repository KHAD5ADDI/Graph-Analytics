Table~\ref{tab:brokers} compares the leading ingredients under our two
distance choices. With hop distances, Water, Salt and Butter lead the
ranking. Across all ingredients, the Spearman correlation between frequency
and hop betweenness is $\rho=0.968$. Degree also follows frequency
($\rho=0.955$). Common ingredients are the path intermediaries in this
graph. We read these correlations descriptively, because frequency and
edges come from the same recipes.

With $1/\mathrm{PMI}$ distances, the leaders become Couscous, Lasagna,
Shallot, Coconut and Saffron. The Spearman correlation with frequency falls
to $\rho=0.323$. We interpret these ingredients as brokers under this
distance choice only. Stronger PMI associations give shorter steps, so
these shortest paths need not use the most frequent ingredients.

\paragraph{Inspecting individual ingredients.}
Community numbers refer to Table~\ref{tab:communities}. Water occurs in $10{,}128$ recipes and has degree $189$. It ranks $1$ by
hop betweenness but $332$ by $1/\mathrm{PMI}$ betweenness. Of its neighbours,
$72$ are inside its community $1$ and $117$ are outside. Its strongest PMI
partners are Yeast ($1.67$), Meat bouillon ($1.54$), Gelatin ($1.48$), Gin
($1.47$) and Bulgur ($1.45$). Yeast, Gelatin and Gin belong to community $1$;
Meat bouillon and Bulgur belong to community $0$. These neighbours connect
Water to baking, setting mixtures, drinks, broth and grain ingredients.

Shallot occurs in $2{,}101$ recipes and has degree $155$. Its ranks are $31$
with hops and $3$ with $1/\mathrm{PMI}$ distances. It has $77$ neighbours
inside community $0$ and $78$ outside. Its strongest PMI partners are
Chanterelle ($3.34$), Chervil ($3.06$), Oyster mushroom ($2.95$), Madeira wine
($2.95$) and Cognac Brandy ($2.87$). These links connect Shallot to mushrooms,
a herb and cooking alcohols. Oyster mushroom belongs to community $3$;
the other listed partners belong to community $0$.

Couscous occurs in $245$ recipes and has degree $54$. It ranks $175$ with
hops and $1$ with $1/\mathrm{PMI}$ distances. It has $28$ neighbours inside
community $0$ and $26$ outside. Its strongest PMI partners are Turnip
($3.96$), Vegetable Broth ($3.58$), Apricot ($3.53$), Butternut squash ($3.44$)
and Currant ($3.30$). The vegetables and broth belong to community $0$;
the fruit belongs to community $1$. Couscous thus links vegetable and
broth ingredients with fruit.

The ingredient graph has no articulation point and no bridge, so no single
ingredient or edge holds it together. Betweenness therefore measures how
much an ingredient brokers paths, not a single point of failure.
