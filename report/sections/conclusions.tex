We find communities in both graphs, but the connecting ingredients we
identify depend on the distance used for betweenness. The ingredient
graph has $362$ nodes and $10{,}853$ edges in one connected component.
Louvain gives five communities of $112$, $97$, $57$, $55$ and $41$
ingredients, with modularity $0.400$. They group vegetables and herbs,
sweet and baking ingredients, salad and sauce ingredients with beef and
pork, ginger, soy sauce and fish, and spices and legumes. The number of
communities is five in $28$ of $30$ seeds. Water, Salt and Butter lead hop
betweenness. Across all ingredients, hop betweenness closely follows
frequency, with Spearman correlation $\rho=0.968$. With $1/\mathrm{PMI}$
distances, Couscous, Lasagna and Shallot lead instead, and the correlation
with frequency is $\rho=0.323$. Common ingredients are the brokers when
every edge counts as one step, while strongly associated but less
frequent ingredients become brokers when stronger association means a
shorter step. The graph has no
articulation point or bridge, so removing a single ingredient or edge
does not split it.

For countries, we find a partial agreement between communities and UN
regions. The graph has $171$ nodes and $2{,}066$ edges in five components,
with $166$ countries in the largest. Louvain gives $11$ communities and
modularity $0.286$. An all-Asian community and a mostly Oceanian community
form clear regional blocks. The large communities also mix Europe with
the Americas and Africa with the Americas. We measure agreement as the
share of countries whose region is the most frequent in their community.
This share is $64.3\%$, compared with a mean of $34.0\%$ over $1{,}000$
shuffles ($p\approx0.001$). The regional comparison gives evidence of an
association between ingredient similarity and UN regions. The mixed
communities show that these labels do not describe the whole partition.
Across thresholds, modularity rises partly because the graphs split
into more components, so it cannot choose the thresholds on its own.

Our data and methods limit these findings. CulinaryDB is a sample of
recipes with an imbalance towards US and Italian cuisines, so it does
not represent cuisines evenly. Country profiles are small, and a few
ingredients decide whether an edge passes the Jaccard threshold. Louvain
depends on the random seed. Over $30$ seeds the ingredient graph has $4$
to $6$ communities and modularity between $0.389$ and $0.402$, so the main
groups are stable, but the reported partition is one of several possible
results.
For weighted betweenness, we use only $1/\mathrm{PMI}$
as a distance choice; another conversion could give different rankings.
The shuffle test treats countries as exchangeable when it permutes
region labels. This assumption does not account for dependence between
neighbouring countries.

Future work could use a more balanced recipe sample and fuller country profiles.
We could compare Louvain with label propagation, try other conversions
from PMI to distance, check the ingredient communities against the cuisine
labels, and use shuffles that preserve some regional dependence.
