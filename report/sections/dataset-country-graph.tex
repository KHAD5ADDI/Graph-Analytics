Each of the 171 countries and territories is a node. The profile of a country
is the set of ingredients that appear in at least one of its dishes, that is,
the union over all its dishes. For two countries with profiles $A$ and $B$,
the weight is the Jaccard similarity, which the course also uses in its
lab~\cite{quercini2026}:
\begin{equation*}
  J(A,B) = \frac{|A \cap B|}{|A \cup B|},
\end{equation*}
the share of the ingredients of the union that both countries use. We keep an
edge when $J \geq 0.30$, which gives 2{,}066 edges. We also tried a profile
made of the national dish alone. It has about ten ingredients per country, so
the similarities are too coarse, and we keep all the dishes. Profiles remain
small: the median profile has 16 ingredients, and 20 countries have 8 or
fewer. One ingredient can therefore decide whether an edge passes the
threshold. We discuss the choice of the threshold with the sensitivity
analysis.
