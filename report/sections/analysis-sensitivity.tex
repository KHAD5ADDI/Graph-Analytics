We rebuild both graphs over a grid of thresholds. For ingredients, we cross
minimum co-occurrence counts of $5$, $10$, $20$ and $50$ recipes with PMI
thresholds of $0.0$, $0.5$ and $1.0$. For countries, we test Jaccard thresholds
of $0.15$, $0.2$, $0.25$, $0.3$, $0.35$, $0.4$ and $0.5$. We track connected
components, average clustering, the number of Louvain communities and
modularity. Clustering measures how often a node's neighbours are also
neighbours of each other. We choose minimum co-occurrence $10$, PMI threshold $0.0$ and
Jaccard threshold $0.30$ as a compromise: the ingredient graph stays
connected and the country graph mostly connected, while both show community
structure. These choices depend on the tested grid. Section~\ref{sec:res-sensitivity}
presents the comparisons.

\paragraph{Shuffle test.}
If country communities had nothing to do with geography, randomly
reshuffling the UN region labels would match them about as
well as the real labels do. Modularity measures how well communities divide
the graph; it does not measure agreement with an outside label. We define
agreement as the share of countries whose UN region is their community's
most frequent region, with the region labels of Section~\ref{sec:sources}. We keep the Louvain communities fixed, using seed
$42$, and permute the region labels of the $171$ countries $1{,}000$ times.
We recalculate each community's most frequent region after each shuffle.
With $A_{\mathrm{obs}}$ the observed agreement and $A_b$ the agreement after
shuffle $b$, we calculate
\[
p = \frac{1 + \#\{b : A_b \geq A_{\mathrm{obs}}\}}{1 + 1{,}000}.
\]
Here $\#$ counts shuffles meeting the condition. We treat region labels as
exchangeable, meaning that any assignment preserving the region counts is
allowed. Neighbouring countries are not independent, which limits this
assumption. A match shows association, not cause.
